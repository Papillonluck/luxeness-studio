01

Why not just use AI?

One 2026 analysis of 5,000 LinkedIn posts found around eight in ten were likely written by AI,* and readers have learned to spot them. LinkedIn has even added a ‘Seems like AI slop’ option,** so people can report those posts and hide them from their feed. When a leader publishes the same generic content as everyone else, it doesn’t build trust, it quietly costs it.

AI can write from anything you feed it, but it can’t judge what should be published and what shouldn’t: which stories build your reputation, and which are better left unsaid. Technology can support the writing, but only if what you’re saying is worth sharing. That judgement and thinking are what I bring, and every post is written in your voice with experience only you have, and published with your approval.

I do use AI, but only where it makes the work more efficient: organising your interviews and voice notes, pulling out themes and lessons, arranging the material and producing a rough first draft, and keeping your second brain linked and up to date, cross‑referencing everything you’ve ever shared far faster and more thoroughly than could be done by hand. It never does the thinking, and nothing it produces is published as it stands. The thinking is yours, and I rewrite and edit every post until it’s ready to publish.

I’m the editor‑in‑chief, not AI.

02

Why not your comms, PR or social media team?

People buy from people, not companies. Your company page shares what the business is doing: openings, launches, appointments and awards. It’s where people go for news. Your profile is where they go to understand the person making the decisions behind the brand: how you think, what you’ve learned and where you’re taking the business next.

A company page can’t build relationships either. It can’t comment on an owner’s post, reply in your voice or start a conversation with a partner. Only a person can, and that’s where opportunities begin.

Your social media team speaks for the brand, and PR earns coverage for the business. Writing for the leaders shaping it is a newer and different craft, personal to you rather than the company. The AI and tech organisations that take it most seriously now give their C‑suite a dedicated executive content writer, whether in‑house or external. It also benefits from an outside view, because from the inside it can be hard to see what sets you apart, or which parts of your experience others would value most.

This doesn’t replace your company page, your team or your agency. I work within your brand guidelines and any social media policy your company already has, and alongside your communications team, so they can see every post before it goes live and your voice supports theirs. It gives them sharper material to work with, and everything you publish lifts the business with it.

03

Why not a generalist ghostwriter?

Luxury runs on trust and attention to detail. One generic phrase, or a detail that doesn’t ring true, and the people you most want to reach stop reading. Get it right, and they notice that too.

A generalist has to learn the industry before they can understand your thinking, so your 15 minutes each week go on explaining the basics instead of your insights. I already know it: hospitality economics, investment priorities, how luxury wellness is bought and sold, and the buyers and partners you want to reach. Nothing gets lost in translation, and every conversation goes straight to what only you know.

The foundation · Weeks 1 to 4
Building your digital reputation right from the start

This is where your personal brand and position as a thought leader are built by a specialist in luxury wellness and hospitality: a business strategist, not just a digital writer. It covers the point of view and reputation you want to be known for, and the groundwork every post is written from.

Each week builds on the one before: your story first, then your positioning, then your voice and second brain. In the final week your LinkedIn profile is rebuilt to reflect all of it, your flagship post goes live, and your first two weeks of posts are approved and ready to schedule for publishing.
So from your very first post, you’re seen as an authority.

Without this foundation, even well-written posts go unnoticed, because there’s no clear strategic positioning behind them.

Questions

What leaders ask beforetrusting me with their name.

Will it actually sound like me, not AI?

It starts with you. During the foundation, I build a voice guide from your interview and from what you’ve already written and said in public: your phrases, your tone and the stories you return to. If you’ve never published anything, it makes no difference: the way you speak in your interview and voice notes captures exactly how you sound in person.

AI helps with the workflow, including a rough first draft, but never the thinking. Every post starts from your own words and stories, and I rewrite and edit each one myself as editor‑in‑chief, checking it against your voice guide, so nothing reads like generic AI content. Everything you tell me stays private and is never used to train a shared or public AI model.

We don’t reach the publishing stage until your posts sound like you and you’ve agreed them.

How much of my time does this take?

About 20 minutes a week: 15 minutes to share your thinking, on a short call or by voice note, whichever suits you that week, and about 5 minutes to approve your three posts once your voice is set. It takes a little longer in the first few weeks, and gets quicker the longer we work together. On Signature, allow a little longer to approve your newsletter.

Quick thoughts sent whenever they strike are welcome too, and interviews and podcast appearances you’ve already given become posts. Everything else, from writing to scheduling and publishing, is handled for you, and you never need to draft a word.

Why a six-month commitment?

A credible body of thought leadership is built through consistency, not a few isolated posts. Recognition starts with your first post and grows from there, but a reputation is earned over seasons, not weeks.

It takes around six months to build real momentum, which is why every engagement starts there. That’s the time your thinking needs to become what the industry knows you for.

Who writes my posts?

I will. I run your interview, build your strategy, and shape and edit every piece myself, and if your plan includes replies, I write those too, always in your voice.

Is this personal branding?

Yes. It builds your personal brand and your position as a thought leader, published on LinkedIn and, with Signature, in your own newsletter too. It comes from your judgement and experience, so it reads as the thinking of a leader, never as self-promotion.

What if I don’t like a post?

Nothing is published without sign-off from you or your communications team. You can ask for changes or hold a post back, and if it still isn’t right, perhaps because the angle is wrong, I write a fresh one to replace it, so you never lose a post from your month.

Your feedback also shapes what I write next.

Will people know I use a ghostwriter?

Not from me. I never name the leaders I write for, unless you ask me to.

And no one will tell from the writing itself. It comes from your thinking and the way you speak, so the leader people read online is the same one they meet in person.

I’ve tried LinkedIn before and got nowhere. How is this different?

Most LinkedIn advice is written for creators chasing engagement. This is for leaders growing trust and reputation. It starts by capturing your thinking before anything is published, so every post sounds specific, credible and commercially useful, never like an influencer.

From there, results come from being visible week after week. Expect around six months to build momentum, and twelve months before the business results become steady. Because every post is written and published for you, that never depends on how busy your diary is.

I don’t have many followers. Does that matter?

No. LinkedIn no longer shows your posts only to your followers. It shows them to people outside your network too, based on what each post says and how readers respond. So a leader with 100,000 followers can see a post barely travel, while a leader with 1,000 can reach far beyond their own network with the right one. Reach comes from what you publish, not from how many people follow you today.

The stronger your thinking, the further it travels. When people save, share and comment on a post, LinkedIn shows it more widely, which is why becoming a thought leader in your niche is the fastest way to a new audience.

And if you want to grow it faster, that’s what Managed Executive Authority is for. While your posts are published, it builds your audience among the right people in your industry.

Can I skip the foundation?

No. Every post is written from the strategic positioning, voice and second brain built in those first four weeks. Without them, your posts would read as generic, and it would take months before they sounded like you or said what you want to be known for.

With it in place, your thinking reads with authority from your very first post.

What happens after six months?

Your engagement renews for a further six months at the same rate, and your thought leadership keeps growing. The second six months is when the momentum you’ve built starts turning into consistent business results.

If you’d rather take it on yourself, you can choose not to renew, and everything we’ve built is yours to keep: your strategy, your voice guide and every post published in your name.

Do you guarantee results?

I guarantee everything in the tier you choose, completed in full every month and in your voice, with nothing published without your approval.

What I can’t guarantee is what’s outside anyone’s control, like the LinkedIn algorithm, or exact numbers of followers, leads or sales. Those depend on far more than what you publish, which is why the work is built to grow your reputation with the people you most want to reach, not to chase numbers.

Who owns the content?

You do. Your thinking, your second brain and everything published in your name belong to you: every post, your profile and, on Signature, your newsletter and subscriber list. If we ever stop working together, it’s all handed over to you.

Will you send connection requests for me?

No, and that’s what protects your account. LinkedIn’s approved tools can’t send connection requests on anyone’s behalf, and the shortcuts that do are exactly what gets accounts restricted.

On Managed and Signature, I hand-select the people most worth knowing, the owners, investors and partners you could do business with, and send the list to your phone. Sending them takes a few minutes a week, and because every request comes from you, each connection starts as a relationship rather than an automated invite.

Can other partners or C-suite leaders join?

Yes, at any stage. Every leader goes through their own foundation, interview and voice guide, because a CMO’s positioning and voice are never the same as a CEO’s. Each becomes known for their own expertise, and together they show the depth of leadership behind the business.

Can my team be involved in approvals?

Yes. Your PR, legal, compliance or communications team can review content before it’s published. We agree one person with the final say, so approvals stay quick and instructions never conflict.

I also work alongside your PR or communications team, and within any brand guidelines or social media policy your company already has, so your posts support their announcements and everything you publish tells one consistent story.
