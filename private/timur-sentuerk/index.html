What your record already says

Few leaders in our industry have a story that runs from the floor of Claridge’s to the boardroom. Yours does.

  • 01

    From commis waiter to CEO, in 35 years.

    In 1991, fresh from your apprenticeship in the Black Forest, you joined Claridge’s as a commis waiter. Since then: The Ritz-Carlton, Mandarin Oriental, Oberoi, Langham in Hong Kong, Pan Pacific in Singapore, and Kempinski in Geneva, where you led more than 65 hotels and residences across 30 countries. Alongside it, a decade as a consultant with The Ritz-Carlton Leadership Center, developing other leaders.

    From your LinkedIn profile and your Claridge’s post.

  • 02

    From 13th to 3rd, in a single year.

    Under your leadership, The Doyle Collection rose from 13th place (63.3%) to 3rd (91.9%) in the Global Hotel Alliance Measurement Matrix, reaching the global top three for the first time in a 17-year partnership, and winning Most Improved Brand Performance 2026.

    From your post on the award.

  • 03

    Recognised for both its hotels and its people.

    The Westbury named the number one hotel in Dublin and The Dupont Circle the third in Washington, DC, in Travel + Leisure’s World’s Best Awards, alongside Best Workplaces recognition in Ireland and the UK.

    From your posts and The Doyle Collection’s.

What LinkedIn shows today

Your record is stronger than your presence. Here is what a peer, an owner or an investor sees when they look you up.

  • 01

    Your most personal post did as well as your biggest award.

    Your return to Claridge’s, and your thanks to Rainer Burkle, drew 263 reactions. The GHA award, the headline result of your year, drew 266. A story only you can tell travels as far as the result everyone else can report.

    From your posts, as of 11 November.

  • 02

    Four posts in five months.

    Most of them celebrate an award or the team, which matters, but they rarely share how you think. Between the GHA award and Claridge’s, around two months passed without a post.

    From your recent activity.

  • 03

    Your profile describes a role, not a point of view.

    “Driving Strategic Growth & Operational Excellence” could describe any senior executive. Nothing yet says what you believe about luxury hospitality, or why your view is worth following.

    From your headline and About section.

  • 04

    The audience is already there.

    More than 13,000 people follow you. The hardest part, the network, is built. What it needs now is a reason to hear from you every week.

    From your profile.

What you could be known for

Each idea ends on a question rather than an answer, because the answer is yours. Finding it is what our conversations would be for. The first two are the ideas you liked yesterday.

    Rainer Burkle took a chance on you at Claridge’s in 1991, and Danilo Larini wrote under your post that you became that person for him in Bangkok. How do you now decide who gets that opportunity at The Doyle Collection, and why does it sit behind your Great Place To Work recognition?

    From your Claridge’s post, its comments and your Great Place To Work post.

    Why independent luxury matters.

    Having led across international luxury hospitality, from The Ritz-Carlton to Kempinski, you now lead a family-owned collection. What makes it distinctive, particularly for its people and its guests, and what can it offer that a global brand cannot?

    From your career history and The Doyle Collection’s own description.

  • 03

    The turnaround.

    A family-owned collection moved from 13th to 3rd in a year. Owners and investors will want to know what changed, and what you decided in the first months that others in your position would not have. What was the one decision that made the difference?

    From your GHA award post.

  • 04

    Four continents, three cities.

    You have led hotels from Hong Kong and Singapore to Geneva, and now lead them in Dublin, London and Washington. What did Asia teach you that European luxury is only now learning?

    From your career history.

  • 05

    Number one in your own city.

    The Westbury was named the number one hotel in Dublin in Travel + Leisure’s World’s Best Awards, and Ireland’s number one in Condé Nast Traveler’s Readers’ Choice Awards. Reaching the top is one thing. What does it take to stay there, in the city that knows you best?

    From your posts on both awards.

    Standards, without the script.

    Your profile describes bringing Forbes standards together with a signature service culture. Guests notice standards when they slip, and warmth when it is genuine. How do you hold both, without hospitality starting to feel scripted?

    From your LinkedIn profile.

  • 07

    What can’t be taught.

    From your LinkedIn profile.

How it would work

You already do the hardest part: you have the record, the network and the stories. LinkedIn adds the thinking behind them, shared every week with the owners, investors and peers who shape our industry. I write that for you, in your voice.

It also takes less of your time than you might think. We would begin with your Authority Foundation: one 45-minute conversation to set your story, positioning and voice. Then come two reviews of no more than 20 minutes each, and within a week your LinkedIn profile would be optimised to establish you as an authority and grow your following.

From then on, it is around 20 minutes a week, and you will never face a blank page: the research, ideas, thinking and drafting are mine, and you simply confirm that each piece reflects your view.

I would love to hear which of these ideas feels most like you.
